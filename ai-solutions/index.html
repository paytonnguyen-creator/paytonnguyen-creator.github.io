About us

Every company now has an AI question. Most don't have anyone to answer it.

The gap isn't models — it's judgment. Which problem is actually worth automating? What does the data support? What breaks when a language model is wrong 4% of the time, and is that acceptable here?

We founded AI Solutions @ Berkeley to put students on the right side of that question. Each semester we assemble small, cross-disciplinary teams — engineers alongside people from economics, cognitive science, design and business — and hand them one scoped problem from a real organization. The team owns it end to end: discovery, data, prototype, evaluation, handoff.

We are not a lecture series and we are not a case-competition club. The deliverable is a working system and the evidence that it holds up.

Our bar for a finished project: the partner still uses it a month after we leave.

  • Interdisciplinary by design An AI deployment fails on adoption and incentives far more often than on architecture. We staff for both.
  • Real scope, real constraints Partner data, partner deadlines, partner definition of done — not a sandbox dataset with a tidy answer.
  • Evaluation is the deliverable Anyone can demo a prompt. We measure the thing, document where it fails, and say so plainly.
  • Teaching built in Internal workshops each semester so a first-year with no ML background can contribute by week four.

How a semester runs

Sixteen weeks, one problem, one working answer.

Consulting hours are the same as anyone's: the difference is what you do in weeks one and two. We spend them narrowing the question until the build is obvious.

Bring us a problem
Weeks 1–2

Scoping

Stakeholder interviews and a written problem statement. If we can't state the success metric in one sentence, we haven't scoped it yet.

Weeks 3–5

Discovery & data

Process mapping, data access, a baseline. Often the most valuable week is the one where we prove the naive approach already works.

Weeks 6–10

Build

Two-week sprints with a partner check-in closing each one. Something demoable exists by week eight, whatever else slips.

Weeks 11–13

Evaluation

Held-out tests, failure taxonomy, cost and latency under real load. This is where we find out what we actually built.

Weeks 14–16

Handoff

Final presentation, documented repository, and a runbook. Plus a candid memo on what we'd do differently with more time.

For companies & labs

Bring us a problem you haven't had time to look at.

Our engagements are pro bono. What we ask for instead is a real scope, a point of contact who can answer questions inside a week, and access to whatever data the work requires.

The best first projects tend to be the ones sitting in someone's backlog: a manual process nobody has costed out, a pile of unstructured documents, a support queue, an internal tool that never got built. Startups, research groups, campus units and non-profits are all welcome.

FAQ

Questions we keep getting

Do I need to know machine learning to apply?

No. Roughly half of any good project team is scoping, interviewing, analysis and communication. If you're joining as a builder we do expect real programming experience, but "I've used an LLM API" is not a prerequisite for anyone.

What's the time commitment?

Plan on 6–8 hours a week during the semester: a team working session, a partner check-in, and your own project work. It climbs in the weeks before a deliverable and drops after.

Are you a technical club or a business club?

Both, deliberately. The failure mode of a purely technical club is building something nobody asked for; the failure mode of a purely business one is recommending something nobody can build. We staff teams so neither happens.

Can freshmen and transfer students apply?

Yes, and we'd like you to. Founding cohorts skew upperclassman by default and that's a problem for continuity, so year is not a factor in our decisions.

How is this different from the other Berkeley consulting clubs?

Scope and output. Traditional consulting clubs deliver a recommendation deck; we deliver a working system plus the evidence it works. If your interest is strategy consulting recruiting, there are excellent clubs on campus built for that — we're built for shipping.

I'm not a student. Can I still work with you?

Yes — see For companies & labs. We're actively looking for founding-semester partners and we'll happily talk through whether a problem is a good fit before you commit.
