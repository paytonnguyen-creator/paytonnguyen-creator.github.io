Thirty billing dates

Release all ten episodes at once or spread them across ten weeks? Explore the tradeoff between signups and renewals in a simulator built on explicit assumptions, without Netflix subscriber data.

  • Simulated 13,000 households over 182 days, comparing five release patterns with live, adjustable assumptions.
  • Found opposing priorities in the model: concentrated releases favor signups; staggered releases favor renewals.
  • Found the winning margin was smaller than swings caused by the assumptions; showed when changing them reverses the recommendation.
  • Tech: JavaScript, agent-based simulation, Web Worker, hand-drawn SVG

Degree Ledger

A clearer path to graduation. Degree Ledger brings Berkeley's major, minor, college, and transfer requirements into one tracker so students can see what remains.

  • Supports every L&S and CDSS major and minor. Thirteen programs include course lists checked against the Academic Guide; others load from pasted Guide requirements.

Why 62% of customers never came back

What brings customers back? An analysis of repeat purchases that tests whether retention efforts should focus on prediction or a second-purchase program.

  • Analyzed 67,591 real CDNOW orders from 23,570 customers, with a fully observed repeat-purchase window.
  • Found weak predictive value in first orders (holdout AUC: 0.576); statistical controls reduced the strongest apparent effect by 82%.
  • Recommended a timed second-purchase program, with an opportunity estimate and clear stop criteria.
  • Tech: Python, pandas, scikit-learn

The pickleball bottleneck

Less waiting, fewer wasted trips. A court-demand simulation explores how forecasts and rotation rules could improve open play.

  • Simulated 843,581 visits across five parks using four years of real NOAA weather, clearly labeling assumptions and modeled results.
  • Found that 6× the demand produced 9× the wait and 52× the walkaways in the simulation; darkness caused the most wasted trips.
  • Used four congestion states to reflect forecast uncertainty, then recommended a free rotation-rule change that outperformed the app in the model.
  • Tech: Python, pandas, scikit-learn, discrete-event simulation

How I work through a problem.

Four steps guide my work, from defining the decision to making a recommendation that can be tested.

  1. 01

    Frame the real question

    Define the decision, who it affects, and what evidence would change it.

  2. 02

    Interrogate the data

    Clean the data, check assumptions, and test competing explanations.

  3. 03

    Find the mechanism

    Connect the pattern to a plausible explanation of behavior and identify what would disprove it.

  4. 04

    Make the call

    Recommend an action, explain the tradeoff, and define how to measure success.
